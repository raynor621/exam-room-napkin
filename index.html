Building the knee…

Why it won't heal on its own

It hurts, it swells, and then it chills out. After two or three weeks people are walking around like nothing happened.

MCL, the good one
Outside the knee. Heals in every single time on its own.
ACL
Inside the knee, and stuff inside the knee doesn't heal very well.
↓
It stops hurting. What people don't like is that it feels unstable with cutting, pivoting and twisting. You plant and it gives out. "I don't trust this knee."

Most young people don't like their knee without a good ACL, and most of them want to have surgery for it.

What do we make it out of?

Four options. Bottom rung first.

4
Patellar tendon (the gold standard)
The middle part with a piece of bone on each end, fixed with plastic screws that absorb into bone. If I could only use one graft forever, this is it. All my athletes under twenty get it.
3
Quad tendon
The middle of your quad tendon, 65 mm long and 9 mm wide, buttons on each end, nothing hard across a growth plate. Same performance. A month or two longer to get your quad back. The kneeler's choice.
2
Hamstrings
Gone down in popularity. They stretch out and are unpredictable. I don't use them anymore.
1
Cadaver (allograft)
Someone else's tissue. Under twenty-five, a forty percent higher failure rate. I don't like to use them under forty.

Between patellar tendon and quad, there's no wrong answer.

What recovery looks like

Day surgery. Walking on it right away, moving it right away.

Day 0In and out the same day. Nerve block, asleep, home to watch Netflix.
3 to 4 weeksCrutches and a brace to help, but all your weight on it from day one.
6 weeksWalking like a normal person. The danger zone: it feels good, but the graft is weaker now than on day one.
3 monthsEverything's healed. Start jogging, work out like a normal human.
7 monthsFull return to cutting, pivoting and twisting sports. Quad grafts sometimes eight or nine.

You're not bored for seven months. It's a long recovery, but it's a very active recovery. What takes seven months? Your quad gets super puny, and you spend that time getting it back.

When do we do it?

The kid on the sideline wants it tomorrow. I make him wait three weeks for the knee to chill out and calm down.

The surgery is the same if I do it now, six months from now, or a year from now. Go to Disney first. You've not done anything bad by waiting.
↓
Prehab in the meantime, for three reasons.
1
Time for the knee to chill out
Surgery is like another injury. Recovery is faster if the knee is calm and moving first.
2
Get the knee ready
Motion and quad strength going in.
3
The real one: meet your therapist
You spend a lot of time with them afterwards. If they're weird and you don't like them, you can switch and it's not awkward.

I'll do it on a Saturday. I don't care. Three reasons.

1

If the meniscus has a tear

I repair it with stitches, usually all through the scope. All it changes is how I let you walk on it for the first six weeks.

One or two stitches

More than two

Repairing the meniscus instead of taking it out is the thing I'm most proud of. Taking it out is easier, and it's not the right thing to do most of the time.

The quad cycle

When your quad is really strong, the front of your knee just doesn't hurt. The problem is the loop it gets stuck in.

Something in there gets angry.
↓
It swells. The swelling shuts your quad down.
↓
The quad gets weaker, so it hurts worse.
↓
The things that make the quad stronger are the things that hurt, so you can't do them.
↺ and around again

It's not the chondromalacia or the meniscus tear that hurts. It's your body's overreaction: it sends inflammation to fix a problem it can't fix. Turn that off, and sometimes it forgets about it.

3

A prescription-strength version of ibuprofen, every day for a week or two.
4
An injection
It gets it feeling good long enough to go do stuff and get stronger, and when it wears off you don't know it.
5

Absolutely what we do if this isn't getting better. Anything I'd find on it, I would inject before I'd ever operate on it.
6

We have it for some of these things. It's not where we start.

I like to start low and work my way up.

Not a Band-Aid. Your knee has looked like this for years; it just got angry recently. If we can get it un-angry, it goes back to normal.
