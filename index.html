Twenty-five and up. None of them have hip pain.

"Labral tears aren't harming anything by being there. It's just annoying when they keep on hurting."

Having a labral tear is not bad. Having a painful labral tear, that's the thing.

And we have options. Bottom rung first.

Physical therapy alone

Adolescent athletes with painful labral tears and impingement, treated without surgery first (Pennock and colleagues, 2018).

Painful labral tear. Start physical therapy, rest, and change what you're doing.
↓
Most of them
82% never needed surgery. Seventy percent with PT alone, another twelve after one injection.
The rest
Surgery then. Everyone ended up improving about the same at two years.

In the room: seventy-two to eighty-three percent get better without an operation. It's the ones that don't that we go do surgery on.

How deep is the socket?

The center-edge angle on your X-ray, in degrees.

25
Normal
A normal roof over the ball.
20
A little shallow
A labral repair is soft-tissue surgery for a bony problem, so a bit less likely to work. Most people still want to try it first.
18
The floor for a scope
Good studies show a hip arthroscopy still does very well down to eighteen.
14
True dysplasia
A different operation: cut the pelvis and move it over. Not something we do in forty-year-olds.

Shallow sockets grow big labrums. Plenty of tissue to repair.
